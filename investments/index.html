Understanding Robinhood Gold & Strategies

Robinhood Gold ($5/mo or $50/yr) is a premium subscription: higher APY on uninvested cash, lower margin rates, and capped advisory fees on managed accounts.

Robinhood Strategies (launched 2025) is the robo-advisor product: automated portfolios across US large-cap, mid-cap, small-cap, international, emerging markets, and bonds. Accounts under $500 use ETFs only; $500+ may use ETFs and individual stocks. You need both Gold + Strategies for the $250 fee cap. Without Gold, it's a standard 0.25%. On fees alone, Gold + Strategies wins above ~$120K thanks to the cap — but the full picture depends on TLH frequency, direct indexing, and transfer bonuses. What Is Direct Indexing?

Instead of buying an index ETF, direct indexing buys individual stocks to replicate an index. You can sell individual losers even when the overall index is up, creating more tax-loss harvesting opportunities. Research estimates 0.5-1.0% additional pre-tax alpha per year — but only on the U.S. stock sleeve of your portfolio, not the whole thing.

Wealthfront bundles DI into its standard 0.25% robo-advisor fee for accounts over $100K, but it only applies to the U.S. equity portion (~40% of a diversified portfolio). Public offers a pure direct-index product at 0.19% where 100% of assets are direct-indexed. Schwab, Fidelity, and Vanguard offer DI as separate products (not in their robo-advisors) at 0.40%+.

Transfer Bonuses

Most brokerages offer cash bonuses to lure your assets. Some are robo-advisors, others are just brokerages (you'd manage your own portfolio). All have hold requirements.

What is a hold period? When a brokerage gives you a transfer bonus, they require you to keep your assets in their account for a minimum time (the "hold period"). If you withdraw before the hold period ends, you forfeit the bonus (and may need to pay it back). A 5-year hold means you're committing to stay for 5 years. This is how brokerages ensure they recoup the cost of the bonus through ongoing revenue from your account.

Assumptions & Notes

  • TLH does NOT require itemizing deductions. Capital losses offset capital gains directly on your tax return, regardless of whether you take the standard deduction or itemize. Up to $3,000 in net losses can also offset ordinary income per year, with unlimited carryforward.
  • Annual costs include advisory fees only. ETF expense ratios (typically 0.03-0.15%) apply to all platforms and are excluded for fair comparison.
  • TLH methodology: Daily TLH estimates use ~1.5% annual harvesting yield (per Wealthfront's reported data) multiplied by your selected tax rate. Annual TLH uses ~0.8% harvesting yield (model estimate; less published data available than for daily). Example at 37%: daily = ~0.55%, annual = ~0.30%. The daily-vs-annual gap is consistent with J.P. Morgan's finding of ~0.30% additional tax savings from daily vs monthly monitoring. Direct indexing benefit uses 0.75% pre-tax alpha (midpoint of 0.5-1.0% from Aperio/Vanguard research), applied only to the U.S. equity sleeve (~40% for diversified robos like Wealthfront, 100% for pure DI products like Public), then scaled by your tax rate. Public’s DI product includes TLH inherently — no separate TLH line is shown to avoid double-counting.
  • TLH benefits are estimates that vary significantly based on market conditions, your tax bracket, and how long you've held the account. Benefits tend to be front-loaded and represent tax deferral, not elimination. All estimates use the tax rate selected above.
  • Transfer bonuses typically require keeping assets in the account for a specified period (usually 5 years for Robinhood and Public).
  • Betterment's pricing is tiered: $5/mo ($60/yr) for balances under $24K without $200/mo recurring deposits; 0.25% for $24K-$1M; 0.15% for $1M-$2M; 0.10% above $2M. The calculator uses these tiers.
  • Vanguard Digital Advisor's gross fee is 0.20%, but credits typically bring the net fee to ~0.15% for all-index portfolios. We use the ~0.15% net figure.
  • "DIY" assumes buying 2-3 broad index ETFs (e.g., VTI + VXUS + BND) at a no-fee brokerage like Fidelity or Schwab, with no automated rebalancing or TLH.
  • Robinhood Gold is only cheaper than Wealthfront on pure advisory fees above ~$120K. Below that, the $50/yr Gold subscription plus the uncapped 0.25% fee makes Robinhood slightly more expensive. (At $120K: both cost $300/yr. Above that, Robinhood's $250 cap kicks in.)
  • All data sourced from platform websites and financial review sites as of March 2026.
